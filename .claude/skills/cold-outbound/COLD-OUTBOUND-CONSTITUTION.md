# The Cold Outbound Constitution

**A governing mental model for producing high-converting cold outbound — consistently and reproducibly.**

This document encodes the **why** behind cold outbound: the causality that determines whether a message works before a single word is written. It is not a copy template, a swipe file, or a tactics list. Those live in the skills. This sits **on top of** the skills and governs *how they are used and in what order.*

> **Prime distinction this document exists to enforce:**
> The skills own the **HOW** (formats, frameworks, sequences, deliverability, persuasion craft). This Constitution owns the **WHY and the ORDER** (what must be true, decided, and sequenced before the HOW is allowed to run).

**Why this exists.** Copy quality has been inconsistent. Inconsistency is never a talent problem — it is a *process* problem. A great email and a dead email usually differ not in craft but in whether the upstream causal layers were correct. When the process is undocumented, quality depends on who happens to be writing and how inspired they feel that day. A constitution makes quality **reproducible** by fixing the sequence, gating each stage, and forcing the same upstream rigor every time. Reproducibility is the goal. Brilliance is a byproduct of rigor applied in the right order.

**How to deploy.** Place this file at the root of the Claude Project (or as `CLAUDE.md` / a referenced doc in Claude Code). It is the first thing to read on any cold-outbound task. It defers to the skills for execution and tells you when to invoke each.

## Article I — The Prime Directive

**Relevance is the only currency. Everything else is exchange rate.**

A cold message has exactly one job: to make the reader feel seen in a way that is relevant to what follows — so that their brain does not have to work to understand why this message exists, for them, right now. Every variable you will ever tune (positioning, offer, sophistication, personalization, length, framework) is a lever that increases or destroys relevance. None of them matters in itself. They matter only as inputs to relevance.

Two corollaries govern all downstream behavior:

1. **Relevance is manufactured upstream, before any word is written.** By the time you are choosing words, relevance is already won or lost. Words deliver relevance; they cannot create it. (See Article IV, the Causal Hierarchy.)
2. **You do not create relevance — you recognize it.** Relevance already exists in the prospect's world as a tension, a transition, or a moment. The job is to find it and attach to it, not to invent it. This is why a real signal beats a clever line every time.

---

## Article II — The First Principle (the physics of a cold message)

Reduce cold outbound to its irreducible form and this is what remains:

> **A cold message is an uninvited claim on a stranger's attention. It begins with negative equity — the interruption penalty plus zero trust — and can only move positive through relevance.**

This single sentence dictates everything:

- **Negative starting equity.** The prospect had a prior commitment to whatever they were doing. You are rerouting attention away from existing priorities. The rational default is *no.* Your message is not competing against other cold emails; it is competing against everything the reader already cares about.
- **Zero trust.** There is no relationship, no accumulated brand experience, no social proof from their network. This is not a metaphor — it is the literal condition. It is the reason any ask must be *priced for zero trust* (Article VIII, Offer).
- **The only escape is relevance.** Relevance is the force that overcomes both the interruption penalty and the trust deficit simultaneously, because a message that meets a tension the reader already feels is no longer an interruption — it is a recognition.

**The atomic unit is not the email. It is the match** between a real tension in the prospect's world and a credible resolution you can name. If that match does not exist, no email exists that will work. This is the deepest reason most outbound fails: the inputs are wrong before the writing begins.

## Article III — The Standing Council

No campaign is legitimate until it survives five lenses. These are not personalities to perform; they are **mandatory review functions**. Run them in your reasoning before producing anything, and again before shipping. A campaign ships only on a **unanimous verdict.** (The discipline behind this council is the mental-model practice in the `deep-thinking` skill — invoke it when a decision is genuinely hard.)

**1. The First-Principles Thinker** — *"What is irreducibly true here?"*
Strips away convention and tactics. Asks: what tension actually exists in this prospect's world, and can we credibly resolve it? Rejects any element that survives only by inertia or imitation. Guards the Prime Directive.

**2. The Contrarian** — *"What if the default is wrong?"*
Attacks every received practice: that personalization is the lever (it isn't — signal is), that more volume helps (it doesn't — more relevance does), that better copy rescues a campaign (it rarely does — the upstream killed it). The Contrarian holds the **right to not send**: the most powerful move is often to disqualify a segment, kill an offer, or refuse to write until the upstream is true. Also polices the council itself — *the map is not the territory; the market is the only authority.*

**3. The Expansionist** — *"What is the system, not the shot?"*
Zooms out from one email to the durable asset: compounding understanding of a vertical's world — its language, tensions, rhythms, and triggers. Insists that learning be captured so it compounds across campaigns. Thinks in moats (vertical depth, signal portfolios), not one-off wins.

**4. The Outsider** — *"How would someone outside this industry see it?"*
Brings the non-marketer's eye. A behavioral scientist sees cognitive-load and risk perception. An anthropologist sees a stranger entering a house uninvited — manners and reading the room beat cleverness. A product manager sees the email as a product, the prospect as a user, the reply as the activation metric. Keeps the work human, humble, and free of self-referential jargon. Kills anything that sounds like "cold email" to a sophisticated reader.

**5. The Executor** — *"Can this actually be run, the same way, every time?"*
Demands operability. A philosophy that can't be run is worthless. Requires a fixed sequence, gates that must pass, one source of truth per variable, a symptom→cause→fix diagnostic, and explicit handoffs to the skills. The Executor is the guardian of **reproducibility** — the reason this Constitution exists.

**The unanimous verdict they all sign:**
> Relevance is the only currency. It is manufactured upstream, through a fixed causal sequence, before any word is written. Creativity operates only after accuracy is secured, and only in service of relevance. The process — not the writer's talent — is the source of consistency. The market is the only authority, and the system must listen continuously.

## Article IV — The Causal Hierarchy (the DNA)

This is the spine of the entire Constitution. Each layer is a **precondition** for the one below it. Work **top-down, never bottom-up.** A defect at any layer cannot be repaired by any layer beneath it. Most operators optimize the bottom (copy, length) while the defect lives at the top (segment, positioning, signal) — which is why their results are random.

```
1. SEGMENT          Who is reading? Are they even the right person?
        ↓           (Wrong here → nothing below can work.)
2. POSITIONING      Do they have a frame for what we are? What are we, for whom, vs what alternative?
        ↓           (Wrong here → the message has no category to land in.)
3. SOPHISTICATION   How many competing claims have they already heard? (Schwartz 1–5)
        ↓           (Wrong here → right message, wrong altitude — sounds like noise.)
4. SIGNAL / TIMING  Why now? What real tension or transition justifies this message existing?
        ↓           (Wrong here → relevance collapses to "random," not "for me.")
5. OFFER            Is the next step worth it at zero trust? Is the value legible before commitment?
        ↓           (Wrong here → they understand and still say no.)
6. FRAMING          Which angle makes our fixed positioning feel relevant to THIS reader/vertical?
        ↓           (Wrong here → true and clear, but it doesn't connect.)
7. COPY FRAMEWORK   Which structure delivers the message cleanly? (PAS, BAB, etc.)
        ↓           (Structure organizes relevance; it cannot create it.)
8. LENGTH           Falls out of all the above. Length is a symptom of clarity, not a choice.
```

**Reading the DNA:**
- Layers 1–4 are **relevance manufacturing.** They are done *before writing.* They are the subject of the Pre-Flight Protocol (Article V).
- Layer 5 is the **exchange terms** — what you ask in return for the relevance you've earned.
- Layers 6–8 are **delivery.** This is where the skills' craft layers operate — *and only here.*

> **The Executor's gate:** You may not descend to a lower layer until the layer above it has a documented, defensible answer. Writing copy (6–8) before Segment/Positioning/Sophistication/Signal/Offer (1–5) are settled is the single most common cause of inconsistent output. It is prohibited (Article X).

## Article V — The Pre-Flight Protocol (run before a single word)

This is what the user means by "before a single word is written." The output of Pre-Flight is **not copy** — it is a set of **decisions** that make the copy nearly inevitable. (This mirrors the research discipline: *research produces copy decisions, not copy; copy is assembled, not invented.*)

**No copy may be written until every question below has a written, specific answer.** Vague answers are failed answers — vagueness is the tell that the upstream work hasn't been done.

**1. Segment (Layer 1)**
- Who exactly is this for? Define it tightly enough that someone outside it would not recognize themselves.
- What are the disqualifiers — who is explicitly *not* this?
- → Invoke `research-market-analysis` (avatar profiling, Hormozi qualification) if this isn't already known. Do not fix a bad segment with copy.

**2. Positioning (Layer 2)**
- What are we, in one line, in a category the reader already understands?
- What is the true competitive alternative — what would they do if we didn't exist?
- What do we do that the alternative cannot? (If you can't name 3 things, you have a positioning problem, not a copy problem.)
- → Invoke `brand-voice` / `go-to-market`. Positioning is fixed and upstream; it is decided here, not improvised in the email.

**3. Sophistication (Layer 3)**
- How many versions of our claim has this market already heard? (Schwartz Stage 1–5.)
- Stage 1–2 → a direct claim works. Stage 3 → you need a *mechanism* (a unique how). Stage 4 → the mechanism must be proprietary/named. Stage 5 → lead with identity/worldview, not claims.
- **The cardinal sin:** writing Stage-1 copy (a bare outcome claim) into a Stage-4 market that has seen that promise fifty times. It reads as noise because, to them, it is.
- → Invoke `research-market-analysis` (Awareness–Sophistication Matrix).

**4. Signal / Timing (Layer 4)**
- **Why now?** What real event, transition, or tension justifies this message reaching this person at this moment?
- Grade the signal honestly against the hierarchy (Article VIII, Signal). A low-value signal carrying a high-value claim is felt as a fake — the reader senses the gap between the signal's weight and the claim on its back.
- If there is no genuine signal, you are relying on **vertical depth** instead (Article VIII) — confirm the vertical is tight enough to substitute.
- → Invoke `coldiq-gtm` (signal taxonomy: Inbound/Postbound/Bridgebound/Outbound; signal sourcing).

**5. Offer (Layer 5)**
- What is the smallest next step that still moves the relationship forward, priced for *zero* trust?
- Is the value **legible and falsifiable before commitment** — can the reader evaluate the return before spending anything?
- Is it **buyer-centric** (a preview of their value) or seller-centric (a request to serve our funnel)? Only the former survives.
- → Invoke `funnel-architecture` (offer design, Value Equation) for the offer's structure.

**6. Message–Market Fit hypothesis (Article VI)**
- State, in one sentence, the model of the prospect's world this campaign assumes: *"We believe [segment] is experiencing [tension] because [signal], and currently [inadequate alternative], and will value [offer] because [reason]."*
- This sentence is your hypothesis. The market will confirm or correct it. Everything in Sharpening (Article IX) is the testing of this one sentence.

**Pre-Flight verdict:** Run the Standing Council (Article III) against these six answers. If any lens objects, you are not cleared to write. Only on a unanimous pass do you descend to Layers 6–8 and invoke the craft skills.

## Article VI — Message–Market Fit (accuracy over creativity)

**Message–Market Fit is the state in which your model of the prospect's world matches their actual reality.** Not when your copy is clever. When it is *accurate.*

Every first draft is a **hypothesis dressed as a claim.** You *think* you know their pain, their language, their trust threshold, their moment. You don't yet — you know your assumptions. The market is the only entity that knows the truth, and it reveals that truth through behavior, not opinion.

This reframes the entire craft:

- The top-converting email is not creative or impressive. It is **accurate** — accurate about the pain, the internal language, the trust level, and the moment. That accuracy is almost never available at the start. It is earned (Article IX).
- "Copy is assembled, not written." The highest-converting language is the prospect's *own* language, mined and rearranged — not your language about them. The further your words are from the words already in their head, the harder their brain has to work, and the more the message reads as a pitch instead of a mirror.
- → The raw material for accuracy — verbatim VOC language, the core emotional complex, the One Belief — is produced by `research-market-analysis`. Accuracy is not an act of inspiration; it is an act of excavation.

---

## Article VII — The Role of Creativity

Creativity is not banned. It is **sequenced and subordinated.**

- **Creativity is forbidden as a substitute for accuracy.** A clever line on top of a wrong segment, a dead offer, or a mis-graded sophistication level is lipstick on a corpse. Out-of-the-box thinking applied to the wrong box produces confident failure.
- **Creativity is mandatory after accuracy is secured** — and only in service of relevance. Once Layers 1–5 are correct, craft and surprise are what separate a competent email from a top-1% one: the pattern-break that earns the first sentence, the unexpected framing that makes a familiar tension feel freshly seen, the line that sounds like a smart friend rather than a vendor.
- **Where creativity legitimately lives:** the *framing* (Layer 6) — choosing a surprising-but-true angle into fixed positioning — and the *craft* (Layer 7–8) — voice, rhythm, the pattern interrupt. This is the domain of `elite-copywriting`.
- **The Outsider's test for creativity:** does the creative choice make the *reader* feel understood, or does it make the *sender* feel clever? Only the former is allowed. Cleverness that serves the ego of the writer is a defect.

> Accuracy is the engine. Creativity is the paint. Painting a car with no engine produces something that looks good and goes nowhere.

## Article VIII — The Variables, and Why Each Behaves As It Does

This Article records the **causal mechanism** behind each lever, so that decisions are made from understanding, not imitation.

### Positioning vs Framing — *not the same thing*
- **Positioning is what you ARE.** The stable, strategic claim about your category, your differentiation, who you're for, and why you over the alternative. Upstream, relatively fixed, foundational. (Layer 2.)
- **Framing is HOW you present what you are** to a specific reader in a specific moment — the lens that makes fixed positioning feel immediately relevant to *them.* Tactical, contextual, dynamic. (Layer 6.)
- **The relationship:** Positioning is the substance; framing is the entry point. Same positioning, many frames — a VP Sales and a CRO get different angles into the identical product. Framing without solid positioning is persuasion without truth (it may get a reply, never revenue). Positioning without good framing never lands (it sits in your deck). They are **sequential, not interchangeable.**
- → Positioning: `brand-voice` / `go-to-market`. Framing-into-copy: `elite-copywriting`.

### Market Sophistication — *the most under-respected variable*
The market's sophistication determines **how much you must explain and how differently you must say it.** It is the altitude setting for the entire message. Most copy fails because it is pitched at the wrong altitude — Stage-1 directness into a Stage-4 market. As a market climbs, bare claims die, mechanisms become mandatory, mechanisms must become proprietary, and finally only identity and worldview cut through. (Schwartz 1–5; see Pre-Flight step 3.)

### Offer — *a trust-weighted value exchange*
The prospect's brain runs one implicit calculation: *"Is the value of yes legible enough, believable enough, and large enough relative to the cost of being wrong?"* Therefore:
- **Legibility is everything.** The brain cannot evaluate vague value. Specificity wins not because it impresses but because it makes the claim **falsifiable in principle** — which signals you believe it's true. Vagueness reads as hiding.
- **Price for the trust that exists (zero), not the trust you wish existed.** "Book a demo" fails structurally: it is a large ask priced for a relationship that doesn't exist. The right ask is the smallest step that still moves forward.
- **Buyer-centric, not seller-centric.** Bad offers ask the prospect to serve your funnel ("let me show you our platform"). Good offers preview *their* value before they commit. Making value legible *before* the ask is the entire game.
- **It must clear the interruption penalty** — which loops back to Signal: an offer attached to a real trigger clears it because it meets an existing tension; an offer sent regardless of timing competes against everything else they care about and usually loses.
- → `funnel-architecture` (offer structure, Value Equation).

### Personalization vs Signal — *why AI openers work sometimes and fail often*
- A first line has one job: **earn the right to the next sentence** by creating a credible bridge between the reader's world and your claim.
- **Observation ≠ insight.** "I saw you raised a Series B" is observation — data anyone can pull. "Your Series B means pressure to 3x pipeline before the next raise, and most teams hit a capacity ceiling there" is insight — it shows you understand what the fact *means.* Insight builds micro-trust; observation just proves you own a tool.
- **Why it fails:** the sophistication arms race has made generic personalization a *spam* signal to good prospects; the opener often doesn't connect to the pitch (human intro, robot pitch — and the research reads as a Trojan horse); flattery masquerades as personalization (makes the *sender* seem friendly, not the reader understood); the "so what" gap (accurate but pointless); right personalization on the wrong target (personalization cannot fix positioning).
- **The resolution:** personalization is only valuable when it is **signal-based**, because signal is relevance and relevance is what's being purchased. Signal answers *"why now?"*; random personalization only answers *"who are you?"* — and *why now* is far more powerful because it meets the reader inside a moment they're already living. **The personalization isn't the point. The signal is the point. Personalization is just how you surface it.**
- → Signal logic and taxonomy: `coldiq-gtm`. Doing AI personalization *reliably at scale* (Clay AI columns, reply classifiers, grounding, evals): `ai-prompting-workflows`.

**The Signal Hierarchy** (grade every signal honestly):
- **High value** — active pain or change: new leadership, rapid hiring in a function, market expansion, product launch, a competitor being acquired. The prospect is *already in motion*; you attach to momentum.
- **Medium value** — potential future pain: a funding round, a job posting, a stated strategic priority. Real, but inferential.
- **Low value** — activity without tension: a LinkedIn post, a podcast appearance, a conference talk. Proves they exist and are active; does *not* prove a problem you can solve now.
- **The failure mode:** mining low-value signals (abundant, easy to scrape) and loading them with high-value claims. The reader feels the weight mismatch.

### Vertical Depth — *relevance without 1:1 personalization*
When a message is written with deep enough **vertical specificity** — the exact internal language, the exact business-model pressures, the exact metrics that vertical cares about — the reader experiences the same outcome as individual personalization: *"this person understands my world."* The mechanism is identical; the path differs:
- **Individual signal → personalization →** "this is for me right now" (1:1 scale, expensive, decays as sophistication rises).
- **Vertical depth → specificity →** "this person gets my world" (1:segment scale, durable, *strengthens* as you refine it).
Extreme segmentation often beats personalization as a *system* because you compound understanding of a world rather than scraping data about individuals. This is the Expansionist's moat.

### Copy Framework & Length — *delivery, not substance*
- **Frameworks** (PAS, BAB, etc.) are structures for *delivering* a message that already works. They organize relevance; they do not create it. A perfectly structured email about the wrong pain still fails. (Layer 7.)
- **Length** is a *symptom of clarity*, never a free choice. It should match the cognitive distance the reader must travel: hyper-targeted + obvious offer → three sentences; any conceptual gap between their worldview and your claim → enough to close it, and not one word more. Every word that doesn't move the reader forward is a word that stops them. (Layer 8.)

## Article IX — The Sharpening Loop (signal-based, not time-based)

A converting email is earned by **sharpening** — and sharpening is the progressive replacement of your guesses with the prospect's truth. It is **epistemological narrowing**: each iteration closes the distance between your assumed model (Article VI's hypothesis) and their actual reality.

**What is actually changing** (it is rarely the copy — it is something upstream of copy):
1. **Your understanding of the real pain** — from the pain that makes your product make sense, to the pain they actually feel, in their words.
2. **The language** — compressing toward their internal vocabulary until the email stops feeling like a pitch and starts feeling like a mirror.
3. **The specificity of the audience** — often the fix isn't the email but *who receives it*; a tighter vertical slice makes the same copy look brilliant.
4. **The offer's calibration to real trust** — discovering what they'll actually risk at zero trust in this context.
5. **The causal thread** — tightening the logical chain (opener → claim → offer) until there are no gaps for the reader to jump. A tight thread feels inevitable; each sentence earns the next.

**Iteration is triggered by signal, not by the calendar.** Time is the wrong trigger — an email does not decay with age; the *accuracy of its assumptions relative to a changing market* decays. The triggers are conditions, not dates:
- **A meaningful drop in a previously-stable metric** (volume unchanged) → the environment shifted; your assumptions are now less accurate.
- **Segment saturation** → you've reached enough of the right people; the remaining pool changed.
- **A change in your own offer/positioning, or a market shift** (new competitor, macro event) → claims no longer match reality.

**The cardinal rule: if it's working, don't touch it.** Iterating on a schedule injects noise into something already accurate. Changing a winner because it's been live 90 days is self-sabotage.

**Diagnose before you change — the data tells you *where* the defect is:**

| Symptom | Where the defect lives | What it implicates |
|---|---|---|
| **Low open rate** | At the door | Subject line / sender credibility / deliverability — not the body |
| **Opens, no replies** | Inside the room | Offer, pain diagnosis, or trust calibration |
| **Replies, but "not now"** | Signal layer | Right person, wrong trigger/timing |
| **Replies, but "we have that"** | Positioning layer | Not differentiated from their current alternative |
| **Calls that arrive confused** | Framing layer | The message didn't make what you do legible |

**Change one variable at a time.** Changing several at once destroys your ability to learn what the market is telling you. **Precision of diagnosis beats speed of iteration** — the best operators aren't the ones who iterate most often; they're the ones who read *which metric moved, by how much, and which upstream assumption it implicates.*

> The whole loop is a **listening posture.** The market is always broadcasting whether your assumptions still hold. The only question is whether you're watching closely enough to hear it before the decay gets expensive. (Capture every learning — the Expansionist's mandate — so accuracy compounds across campaigns instead of resetting each time.)

## Article X — Prohibitions

These are the failure modes the Constitution exists to prevent. Each is a violation of the causal order. The Standing Council treats any of these as an automatic veto.

1. **Writing before Pre-Flight is complete.** No descending to Layers 6–8 until Layers 1–5 have written, specific, defensible answers. (The master prohibition.)
2. **Fixing an upstream defect with a downstream tool.** No rescuing a wrong segment, dead offer, or mis-graded sophistication with copy tweaks. The defect must be fixed at its own layer.
3. **Seller-centric offers.** No "let me show you our platform" / "book a call to explore." Every ask must preview the reader's value and be priced for zero trust.
4. **Low-value-signal personalization.** No loading a scraped LinkedIn post or podcast mention with a high-value claim. Grade the signal; match the claim to its weight.
5. **Wrong-altitude copy.** No Stage-1 direct claims into Stage-4 markets. Grade sophistication first.
6. **Cleverness over accuracy.** No creative flourish substituting for upstream truth. Creativity is permitted only after accuracy is secured and only if it makes the *reader* feel understood.
7. **Calendar-driven iteration.** No changing a working email because time passed. Iterate on signal, never on schedule.
8. **Multi-variable changes.** No changing several elements at once when sharpening. One variable per iteration, so the market's lesson is legible.
9. **Inventing language.** No writing *about* the prospect in your words when their words are available. Assemble from VOC; don't fabricate.
10. **Shipping without the unanimous verdict.** No campaign goes live until all five Council lenses sign.

## Article XI — Integration with the Skill OS

This Constitution is the **why and the order.** The skills are the **how.** Invoke them in the sequence the Causal Hierarchy demands. The Constitution never duplicates a skill; it routes to it.

**Boundary with `go-to-market` (the other orchestrator).** Both this system and `go-to-market` orchestrate the same execution skills, so the division of labor must be explicit. `go-to-market` is the **portfolio layer**: it decides *which* motion to run (founder / community / inbound / product / sales / partner-led), picks the beachhead, and owns the GTM math. This Constitution is the **execution-governance layer for one of those motions — outbound.** The rule: `go-to-market` decides *whether outbound is the right play and where it fits in the mix*; once outbound is the chosen motion, **this system is the front door** that governs how it executes, routing downward to the skills below. Hand back up to `go-to-market` only for motion choice, channel mix, beachhead, or pipeline-math questions — never for the *how* of an outbound campaign, which lives here.

**Invocation order, by layer:**

| Layer | Decision | Skill that owns the HOW |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Segment | Who, and who is disqualified | `research-market-analysis` (avatar, Hormozi qualification, VOC) |
| 2 — Positioning | What we are vs the alternative | `brand-voice` (position→message) · `go-to-market` (positioning strategy) |
| 3 — Sophistication | Altitude of the message | `research-market-analysis` (Awareness–Sophistication Matrix) |
| 4 — Signal/Timing | Why now | `coldiq-gtm` (signal taxonomy, sourcing) |
| 5 — Offer | The exchange terms | `funnel-architecture` (offer design, Value Equation) |
| 6–8 — Framing, Framework, Length | Delivery of relevance | `elite-copywriting` (persuasion craft) · `coldiq-gtm` (cold-email format, sequences, deliverability-safe structure) |

**Two layers that sit beside the message, not inside it:**
- **Does it land in the inbox at all?** Deliverability and sending infrastructure — SPF/DKIM/DMARC, warmup, inbox rotation, domain reputation → `email-marketing`. *(`coldiq-gtm` owns what you say; `email-marketing` owns whether it delivers.)*
- **Does the AI do its job reliably?** Custom AI inside the stack — Clay AI columns, reply classifiers, agentic reply workflows, personalization at scale, grounding/evals → `ai-prompting-workflows`. *(`coldiq-gtm` owns personalization *playbooks*; `ai-prompting-workflows` owns making the AI execute them reliably.)*

**The three-lens triage for any "it broke" symptom** (from `coldiq-gtm`), extended upward by this Constitution: before prescribing, rule out **Play** (content/strategy → `coldiq-gtm`), **Platform** (deliverability → `email-marketing`), and **Prompt** (the AI → `ai-prompting-workflows`) — *and* the upstream causal layers (Segment / Positioning / Sophistication / Signal / Offer), which are this Constitution's domain and the most common true root cause. Use the diagnostic table in Article IX to locate the layer first; only then pick the skill.

**Stack position:**
```
WHY + ORDER     → THIS CONSTITUTION (governs sequence, gates, prohibitions, the Council)
Strategy        → go-to-market (motion, beachhead, positioning, metrics)
Intelligence    → research-market-analysis (segment, sophistication, VOC, One Belief)
Offer           → funnel-architecture (offer design, Value Equation)
Execution       → coldiq-gtm (signal, sequences, cold-email format)
Craft           → elite-copywriting (persuasion, voice, framing-into-copy)
Deliverability  → email-marketing (whether it lands)
AI reliability  → ai-prompting-workflows (Clay AI, classifiers, evals)
```
